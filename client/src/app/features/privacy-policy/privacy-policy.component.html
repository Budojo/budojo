Privacy Policy

Who is responsible for the personal data kept in Budojo, where it lives, and what leaves the device.

Italiano · English

Technical draft — under legal review. The final wording is being reviewed by counsel. The technical facts below (who controls the data, where it is kept, what leaves the device, which third parties the app contacts) match what the app does today. Counsel may revise the wording, not the technical facts.

1. Who controls the data

Each academy that uses Budojo is the data controller of its own data and of its athletes' data. It decides what to record, why, on which legal basis and for how long, and it gives its athletes its own privacy notice.

Budojo is software that runs on the academy's own devices. Budojo and its author receive none of that data and are not a processor of it. This page describes what the software does with the data on those devices, so the academy can describe it in turn.

Questions about the software: privacy@budojo.it (to be confirmed during legal review).

2. Where the data lives

  • On the PC — a Windows app. Everything it stores is in %APPDATA%\Budojo\ on that computer: the database, the documents, the backups and the logs.
  • On the phone — an Android app. Its data is in the app's private storage, excluded from Android's cloud backup and from the transfer to a new phone.
  • No server is operated by Budojo. The hosted version of Budojo has been shut down.

What the app stores, as the academy enters it:

  • The owner's profile — name, email, photo.
  • The academy — name, address, contacts, logo, timetable, prices.
  • Athletes — name, contacts, date and place of birth, codice fiscale, sex as on the document, address, photo, belt and status.
  • Activity — attendance, payments, belt promotions, lesson notes.
  • Documents — medical certificates, ID cards, insurance and other files. Medical certificates are health data (GDPR Art. 9).

3. What leaves the device

  • Nothing reaches Budojo. The app sends no telemetry, analytics or crash reports. Its logs stay on the device.
  • No email and no push. The app sends no email, and the phone's reminders are scheduled on the phone itself.
  • Google, only if the academy connects its Google account — for the backup copy and the sync between PC and phone (§ 4).
  • A backup folder, only if the owner chooses one — the PC copies each backup into it: a USB stick, a network drive, or a folder a cloud service syncs. That service is the academy's own choice.
  • GitHub, for updates — the PC and the phone ask GitHub whether a new version exists, and the PC downloads it from there. GitHub sees the device's IP address and user agent; no academy data is sent.
  • Links the owner opens — a technique's video link or a WhatsApp button opens in the device's browser or app, only when clicked.

Who each one is and what it sees: Sub-processors and third parties.

4. Google Drive: backup and sync

Both are optional and use the academy's own Google account, under the academy's own agreement with Google. Google's processing, and where it happens, fall under that agreement.

  • Backup. Each backup the PC takes (every 6 hours while the app is open) is uploaded to a Budojo folder in that Drive, with the scope drive.file: the app sees only the files it created. An archive holds the database, which is mostly plain text, and the documents, which stay encrypted.
  • Sync between PC and phone. The two devices exchange their data through the same Drive. Every file is sealed with AES-256-GCM under the academy's sync key.
  • The keys. The sync key and the keys that open the documents are kept in the Google account's hidden application data (scope drive.appdata), so the phone can open the gym after signing in with Google.

Whoever controls that Google account can read the gym's data: the database in the backups is mostly plain text, and once the phone is connected, the keys beside it open everything else, medical certificates included. Keep Google's 2-step verification on.

5. Security measures

  • Documents, medical certificates included, are encrypted at rest with the academy's document key.
  • On the PC, the keys, the sign-in token and the Google refresh token are encrypted with the Windows keychain (DPAPI), tied to the Windows user account. The database itself is mostly plain text: access to that Windows account is what protects it.
  • On the phone, the keys are sealed by the Android Keystore, and the app asks for the fingerprint when it opens. Reminders show their full text on the lock screen, names and amounts included.
  • Backup archives carry no keys. Opening the documents on a new computer needs the recovery code.

6. Retention period

  • The academy decides how long to keep its data, and deletes it in the app.
  • On the PC, medical certificates that expired more than 24 months ago are deleted automatically.
  • Removing an athlete takes them off the roster and deletes their documents; their record and history stay.
  • Backups hold about two weeks of history: the six newest, then the last one of each of the 14 most recent days the app ran. What is deleted in the app stays in older backups, on the PC, in the backup folder and on Drive, until they rotate out.
  • Uninstalling does not delete the data. How to remove it: Account deletion.

7. Cookies and storage on the device

Budojo sets no cookies and uses no tracking or profiling. The app keeps a few technical values in its own storage on the device: the language, the theme, the sync's bookkeeping and one view preference; on the phone, also the sign-in token. The full list is in the Cookie Policy.

8. Rights of the data subject

Athletes exercise their GDPR rights (Articles 15–22) with the academy, the controller. Budojo holds none of their data and cannot answer for the academy.

The app helps the academy answer: it shows and corrects an athlete's record, removes an athlete, and on the PC exports the academy's data with its documents as a ZIP file (Settings → Account → Download my data).

9. Complaint to the supervisory authority

Anyone may file a complaint with the competent supervisory authority: for Italy the Garante per la protezione dei dati personali, at garanteprivacy.it.
